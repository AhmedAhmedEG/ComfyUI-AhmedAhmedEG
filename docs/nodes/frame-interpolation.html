Frame Interpolation

Add in-between output frames and increase FPS while preserving duration.

Use it when: Prepare a smoother-rate delivery video after generation.

1. What it does

Duration is preserved. Linear blends frames and can ghost; it is not learned optical-flow motion estimation.

2. Connect it

Decoded frames → images; matching audio → audio
Returned images/audio/fps → encoder

3. First use

  1. Enter the actual input FPS, normally 24 for H3 output.
  2. Choose multiplier and available provider.
  3. Keep cut detection on when the input includes scene cuts.
  4. Encode at the returned FPS, not the original FPS.

Setting

The rate represented by the supplied frames.

Use it when
Match the generated or loaded frame timing before encoding or postprocessing.
What changes
The rate determines playback duration and audio alignment.
Keep in mind
H3 sampling itself runs at 24 fps. Changing an encoder rate does not generate new frames.

multiplier

Setting

Provider and processing controls for decoded output.

Choose an installed provider; enable cleanup only on supported RTX routes; use multiplier/cut detection for interpolation.
What changes
Resizes or adds decoded frames without adding H3 diffusion steps.
Keep in mind
Linear interpolation blends images and may ghost. RTX routes require their own SDK/runtime.

7. Limits & troubleshooting

  • RTX provider needs its supported dependencies and hardware.
  • Using the original FPS after adding frames slows the video and breaks intended timing.
  • Interpolation cannot repair incorrect generated action or anatomy.

8. Check your setup

Confirm the model family, source media and setting scope before comparing output. A visible control is not proof that a missing provider or model actually ran.
